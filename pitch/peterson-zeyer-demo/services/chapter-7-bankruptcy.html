What is Chapter 7 Bankruptcy?

Chapter 7 bankruptcy, also known as "liquidation bankruptcy," allows you to discharge (eliminate) most unsecured debts in exchange for giving up non-exempt property. In Idaho, most people keep all of their belongings due to generous exemption laws that protect your home, car, retirement accounts, and personal property.

Debts Eliminated by Chapter 7

  • Credit card debt
  • Medical bills and hospital debt
  • Personal loans and signature loans
  • Deficiency balances after repossession
  • Business debts (for sole proprietorships)
  • Old utility bills
  • Payday loans and cash advances
  • Most collection accounts

Debts NOT Eliminated by Chapter 7

  • Recent taxes (less than 3 years old)
  • Student loans (with rare exceptions)
  • Child support and alimony
  • Secured debts (if you keep the property)
  • Criminal fines and restitution
  • Debts incurred by fraud

Chapter 7 Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for Chapter 7 bankruptcy in Idaho, you must pass the "means test," which compares your income to the median income for a family of your size. If your income is below the median, you automatically qualify. If above, we analyze your allowable expenses to determine eligibility.

Current Idaho median income levels (updated every 6 months):

  • 1 person: $63,960
  • 2 people: $79,900
  • 3 people: $97,020
  • 4 people: $115,640

Idaho Bankruptcy Exemptions

Idaho's exemption laws are debtor-friendly, allowing you to protect:

  • Homestead: Up to $175,000 in home equity
  • Vehicle: Up to $7,800 per vehicle
  • Personal Property: $16,000 in household goods, furniture, clothing
  • Retirement Accounts: Unlimited protection for 401(k), IRA, pension plans
  • Tools of Trade: Up to $7,800 in work-related tools and equipment
  • Wildcard: Additional $1,600 that can be applied to any property

Timeline and What to Expect

The Chapter 7 process typically follows this timeline:

  1. Pre-filing (1-2 weeks): Document gathering and petition preparation
  2. Filing Day: Automatic stay takes effect, creditors must stop collection
  3. 30-40 days after filing: Meeting of creditors with bankruptcy trustee
  4. 60 days after meeting: Deadline for creditor objections
  5. 90-120 days after filing: Discharge order eliminating qualified debts
